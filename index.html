02 / 11 Formula Student

Battery cell selection & modelling

Goal
Choose the best cells for the team's second EV battery pack.
Challenge
Usable cell models don't exist, and none capture pack geometry, cooling or a real endurance load.
Solution
Built cell and pack models from datasheets, drove them with recorded track data, and compared the finalists in Simscape and Voltt.
Results
20 cells screened · simulation runtime cut 90% · 60% more data behind the decision

Usable cell models don't exist for most candidate cells, and none capture full-pack geometry and cooling. I extracted look-up tables from datasheets, built packs in MATLAB's Battery Builder, and drove them with throttle and brake traces recorded from an FSUK track in Assetto Corsa, reshaped into real power demand.

I ranked the cells against pack targets (under 30 kg, over 5.6 kWh, over 60 kW continuous), then ran the finalists through a full endurance event in Simscape and Voltt, comparing state of charge, temperature rise, voltage sag and peak-power maps. The Samsung 50U and EVE 50PL came out on top; I presented this selection to the judges at FSUK 2026.

I then fitted 2RC equivalent-circuit parameters from virtual pulse tests at nine temperatures (20–60 °C). The resulting tables feed an Extended Kalman Filter for SOC estimation on a 96s4p NMC pack.

  • Battery Builder
  • Voltt
  • 2RC ECM + EKF
03 / 11 Formula Student · Digital twin

Battery & busbar thermal model

Goal
Give the digital twin an accurate battery model that includes busbar and current-path heating, and use it to size the busbars.
Challenge
Cell models ignore the heat added by busbars and welded joints, which grows with current squared and rises as the copper warms.
Solution
An EVE 50PL cell model built on Voltt's P45B Simscape framework, coupled to a copper busbar with temperature-dependent Joule heating, weld-contact heating and convection.
Results
Cell parameters at 9 temperatures (20–60 °C) and 100 SOC points · scaled to the 96s4p pack · busbar and weld heat logged against current to guide busbar design

I extracted 2RC equivalent-circuit look-up tables for the EVE 50PL from Voltt virtual pulse tests and loaded them into the Voltt (About:Energy) ECM block, which originally shipped with Molicel P45B parameters. The digital twin's lap power demand is converted to pack current through the simulated pack voltage, so current rises as the voltage sags, as it does on the car.

The cell's thermal port is coupled to a copper busbar thermal mass. Two heat sources act on it: Joule heating I²R₀(1 + αΔT) with copper's temperature coefficient, and contact heating at the welded cell joints. The busbar loses heat by mixed convection, with the coefficient read from a temperature-dependent table. The model logs cell and busbar temperatures and both heat sources, and runs inside the digital twin as its battery model.

  • MATLAB / Simulink
  • Simscape
  • Voltt (About:Energy)
  • Thermal modelling
04 / 11 Formula Student · Digital twin

Tyre model for the digital twin

Goal
Give the digital twin a realistic tyre model.
Challenge
Raw bench data is noisy and incomplete, with no drive/brake data for our tyre size.
Solution
A nine-stage MATLAB cleaning pipeline and a Magic Formula 6.1 fit, borrowing the combined-slip shape from a similar tyre.
Results
9-stage pipeline · 6 fitted force and moment models · one .tir file read directly by the vehicle simulation

Raw bench data can't be fitted as delivered: it contains aborted runs, transitions between conditions, pressure dips and machine vibration, and there is no drive/brake data at all for our tyre size. I wrote a MATLAB pipeline that cleans it, cuts it into sweeps, groups the test conditions, rejects impure samples and filters the once-per-revolution ripple, then hands SI/ISO data to the open-source Magic Formula Tyre Tool.

For combined slip I borrowed drive/brake runs from an 18×6.0 tyre and scaled its lateral force to match ours at zero slip, so the fit learns only the shape of the grip trade-off, not the other tyre's grip level. A custom parser and start-up checker let teammates re-fit the model themselves.

  • MATLAB
  • Magic Formula 6.1
  • FSAE TTC data
  • Signal processing

07 / 11 Review paper · Research Project course · Not published

Review paper: solid-state battery interface membranes for EVs

Goal
Judge how close solid-state batteries are to EV use, and which electrolyte–electrode interface designs move them closer.
Challenge
Thermal management for solid-state batteries is still immature, so there was little direct research to compare.
Solution
Reviewed electrolyte types and enhancement methods, then compared three original studies on interface membranes against what an EV needs: low heat generation, long cycle life and high C-rate.
Results
Selected the VAMC LLTO membrane: interfacial resistance 853 → 103 Ω·cm² (−85%) and 98% capacity retained after 50 cycles, against 82% for a planar membrane

A single-author review paper, not original research, written in IEEE format for HTU's Research Project course. It has not been published. It compares polymer, oxide and sulfide solid electrolytes, looks at 3D printing, composite hybrid electrolytes, interface engineering and thermal management, and explains why interfacial resistance drives heat generation in a solid-state cell.

The core of the paper analyses three membrane studies. I picked the one that covered every EV requirement adequately rather than the one that topped any single metric.

08 / 11 Capstone · 2025–26

Seawater electrolyzer & degradation model

Goal
Make green hydrogen directly from seawater for an unmanned surface vessel.
Challenge
Chloride ions in seawater pit and destroy standard electrodes within hours.
Solution
A NiFe-phosphate coated anode, a laser-cut test cell, and a Simulink model that predicts electrode wear.
Results
Coated anode lasted 3.5× longer at 20 A · bare electrode lost 36.4% chloride health in the first simulated hour · model matched the analytical solution to 4 d.p.

Seawater destroys ordinary electrodes: chloride ions pit the anode within hours. Our four-person team coated a stainless-steel mesh anode with a NiFe-phosphate layer and built a laser-cut test cell to compare it against a bare electrode.

I built the model. A Simscape electrolyzer (electrical, thermal-liquid and gas networks) feeds current, voltage and temperature into three Arrhenius degradation pathways: kinetic wear, chloride pitting and oxide growth. Their combined health factor is fed back into the circuit as a rising series resistance, so the simulated cell loses output as it degrades.

  • MATLAB / Simulink
  • Simscape
  • Electrochemistry
  • SolidWorks
09 / 11 Venture concept

Virtual power plant dispatch optimiser

Goal
Cut commercial sites' electricity bills by shifting evening peak load onto stored solar.
Challenge
Manual battery dispatch leaves an estimated 15–20% of time-of-use savings unused.
Solution
Specified an hourly mixed-integer optimiser that respects battery limits and is benchmarked against a PV-first baseline.
Results
Targets 8–15% peak-period bill reduction at zero customer capex · exploits a 25–34% tariff gap

I came up with the venture idea and specified the optimiser behind it: an hourly mixed-integer linear program over 24 hours that decides every flow between grid, PV, battery and load to minimise the bill under time-of-use tariffs. It respects state-of-charge and power limits, round-trip efficiency, and never charges and discharges at once. Every run is compared with a PV-first baseline, so savings are measured rather than assumed.

10 / 11 TE Connectivity AI Cup

AI process control for PTFE liner yield

Goal
Raise yield in PTFE catheter-liner manufacturing.
Challenge
Hidden multi-parameter interactions and noisy data that throw up misleading correlations.
Solution
A causal AI workflow with an engineering-validation layer that kept only physically grounded process levers.
Results
−6.4 pp yield loss · ~$2.9M estimated yearly savings · ~46% fewer PTFE failures

With team Vector, I defined the AI workflow and owned its engineering validation. We modelled the process as a causal chain (process parameters → material properties → failure modes), then layered correlation, mediation and random-forest sensitivity analysis. Every significant link was checked against PTFE material science to discard statistical artefacts, leaving one controlled operating procedure.
